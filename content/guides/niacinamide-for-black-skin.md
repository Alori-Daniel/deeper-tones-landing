Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3 that can support your skin barrier and, over weeks, make dark marks and uneven tone look a little fainter [1]. It does not change the colour you were born with, and a higher percentage is not a better one. Give it about 12 weeks, and wear sunscreen every day.

## What niacinamide does

**It supports the skin barrier.** The barrier is the outer layer of your skin that keeps water in and irritants out. In lab and skin tests, niacinamide raised the fats (ceramides) that hold that layer together and reduced water loss in dry skin [2]. In people with eczema, a 2% cream lowered water loss and raised hydration over 4 to 8 weeks [3].

**It may calm oil.** A review reports that it reduces the activity of oil glands, though how it does this is not fully understood [3]. Treat that as a possible bonus, not a promise.

**It helps with uneven tone.** Skin colour comes from pigment, called melanin. Melanin is made in cells called melanocytes, then passed to the surrounding skin cells (keratinocytes) in tiny packets called melanosomes. In lab models, niacinamide slowed that hand-off by 35 to 68% without stopping the pigment being made [4]. Fewer packets reach the surface, so spots can look fainter.

## What the evidence shows for dark marks on dark skin

Here is the honest picture. Most niacinamide trials were small and ran 4 to 12 weeks. Many enrolled Japanese women with sun-related brown spots or tanning, or people with melasma (dark patches, usually on the face), not Black skin with marks left after pimples or razor bumps [1][4].

For post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), the dark mark left after skin is inflamed, the evidence for any treatment is limited. A 2024 review of 48 studies in skin of colour, where 70% of participants were Black, found no treatment with robust results across the board [5]. A 2026 review describes niacinamide as very well tolerated, but modest when used on its own [6].

One small melasma trial of 27 people compared 4% niacinamide with 4% hydroquinone (a strong fading ingredient) over 8 weeks. Both faded the melasma patches. Good-to-excellent improvement was seen in 44% of people with niacinamide and 55% with hydroquinone, and side effects were less common with niacinamide (18% against 29%) [1].

So a realistic aim is modest, steady fading, with niacinamide as one part of a routine. Stopping what irritates your skin and wearing sunscreen come first [7]. [How to fade dark marks on dark skin](/guides/fade-dark-marks-dark-skin) covers the full order of steps.

## Does niacinamide lighten skin?

Many people searching this are hoping for lighter skin, or a fast fix. That wish is understandable, and you deserve a straight answer.

Niacinamide does not change your natural colour. The studies measured dark spots and sun tan. In one early trial in women with facial tanning, skin treated with niacinamide and sunscreen moved back towards its untanned shade after 4 weeks [4]. That is a tan fading, not a new baseline. In another study, the benefit of 5% niacinamide on spots faded over the months after people stopped using it [1]. No routine can change the colour you were born with.

What it can do is help your tone look more even and your marks look calmer. That is a goal worth having.

Be careful with creams that promise a lighter result. Researchers have found steroids or mercury, not listed on the label, in some imported skin products. The American Academy of Dermatology says long-term use of those steroids can leave skin thin, fragile or permanently discoloured [7]. If you are unsure about a product, read [is a glow cream bleaching?](/guides/is-glow-cream-bleaching) and [how to stop using a bleaching cream safely](/guides/stop-bleaching-cream-safely).

## Niacinamide 5% or 10%? Why higher is not better

Strength is the first thing people compare, so here is what the research shows:

- **2%:** in a trial comparing strengths, it did not clearly reduce spots more than plain moisturiser [1]. It has been used to support the barrier in eczema-prone skin [3].
- **4 to 5%:** the strengths used in most spot trials [6]. The 5% strength reduced spots at 4 and 8 weeks [1].
- **10%:** the main review includes no trial of 10% on spots. Its one 10% result was a skin test that found no stinging or flushing [1].

A bigger number on the label has not been shown to work better. Niacinamide is described as well tolerated at the usual strengths below 5%, but reactions vary from person to person [1]. If you are choosing, look for roughly 4 to 5% on the label.

Niacinamide is not the same as niacin (nicotinic acid). Unlike niacin, it does not cause flushing [1].

Irritation is worth taking seriously because irritating products can themselves cause dark spots [7]. If a product burns or stings, stop using it [7]. Add one new active (an ingredient meant to change your skin, such as niacinamide or vitamin C) at a time, so you can tell which product caused a reaction.

## Niacinamide and vitamin C

You may have read that you must never use them together. A 2026 review of treatments for dark marks in skin of colour says niacinamide can work better when combined with vitamin C [6].

The American Academy of Dermatology lists vitamin C among the over-the-counter ingredients that can fade dark spots [7]. If layering them stings, use one in the morning and one in the evening, or stop the one that stings.

For marks that follow pimples, you can also read about [azelaic acid for dark skin](/guides/azelaic-acid-dark-skin).

## How long it takes

Trials measured change at 4, 8 and 12 weeks, and most ran about 8 weeks [1]. A fair plan:

1. Use it daily for about 12 weeks before you judge it.
2. Take a photo in the same light at the start, then every 4 weeks.
3. Keep going if marks are fading, even slowly. Gains can fade if you stop [1].

Remember the marks themselves have their own clock. A mark a few shades darker than your skin usually fades in 6 to 12 months once the cause stops, and deeper marks can take years [7]. [How long dark spots take to fade](/guides/how-long-dark-spots-take-to-fade) explains what changes that timeline.

Sunscreen matters more than any serum. One review, in Asian patients, found that only sunscreen consistently prevented post-inflammatory dark marks, mostly after laser treatment [8]. The American Academy of Dermatology says daily sunscreen can prevent new spots and help clear existing ones [7]. See [sunscreen for dark skin](/guides/sunscreen-for-dark-skin).

## When to see a professional

See a doctor, pharmacist or dermatologist if:

- marks are not improving after about 12 weeks of a gentle routine
- you have spreading redness, pus, fever or severe burning
- a rash, reaction or mark is near your eyes
- a mole or patch has changed
- your skin has become thin or fragile, or bruises easily
- dark patches appeared without a spot or rash first, or look slate-blue or grey
- you are pregnant or breastfeeding and want to add any new active

Some spots need a prescription-strength treatment, and a dermatologist can help you combine treatments safely [7]. If you have used a strong steroid cream for a long time, stopping is best done with a professional's help.

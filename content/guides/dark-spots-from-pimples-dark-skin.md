## Why do pimples leave marks on dark skin?

When a pimple is inflamed (swollen, sore, hot), skin can leave behind a flat tan, brown or grey mark where it healed. The pattern is usually called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, or PIH. "Hyperpigmentation" means extra pigment, the substance that gives skin its colour. This is a pattern, not a diagnosis: a flat mark that stays after a spot has gone often fits it, and a doctor or pharmacist can confirm.

It is common. In one US study of 239 African American people with acne, about 65% developed these marks [2]. A review of treatment studies notes that darker skin tones are more prone to PIH and that it can be hard to treat [5].

How deep the pigment sits matters. A spot a few shades darker than your skin usually takes 6 to 12 months to fade once the cause is stopped [1]. Tan to dark-brown marks sit near the surface and may take months to years to resolve without treatment. Blue-grey marks sit deeper and may be permanent or fade over a very long time [2].

## Step 1: calm the pimples first

New pimples mean new marks. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says that for dark spots caused by a skin condition such as acne, you must treat that condition first [1]. A review in skin of colour agrees that treatment begins with the original inflammation, and that starting early helps marks resolve sooner [2].

For mild acne, the NHS suggests starting with salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide, two acne treatments you can buy over the counter, and asking a pharmacist which suits you [3]. Wash twice a day with a gentle cleanser. Scrubbing irritates the skin and can make acne worse [3][4].

Be patient with acne treatment too. The British Association of Dermatologists says most treatments take 2 to 4 months to work fully, so expect at least 12 weeks before much improvement [4].

## Step 2: leave the spots alone

This is the hardest one, and the most useful. The NHS says not to pick or squeeze spots because this can cause scars [3]. The British Association of Dermatologists adds that it usually aggravates them and may cause scarring and infection [4].

The reason is simple. Marks follow inflammation [2], and picking adds more inflammation on top of the pimple. A flat dark mark can fade. A scar is different: a change in the skin's texture, such as a small dent or a raised bump [4].

If you want a spot to settle, leave it, keep the skin clean and treat it as in step 1.

## Step 3: sunscreen, every day

Sun can darken marks that are already there. A review of skin of colour notes that many people with darker skin do not use sunscreen regularly, and may not realise UV light deepens pigment [2]. A systematic review (a study that pools many studies) found sunscreen was the only measure that consistently prevented new marks [8]. That review covered marks after laser treatment in Asian patients, so treat it as supporting evidence, not proof for acne.

AAD suggests [1]:

- SPF (sun protection factor) 30 or higher, broad-spectrum (covers UVA and UVB rays) and water-resistant
- a non-comedogenic formula if your skin is oily (it is made not to block pores)
- a tinted formula with iron oxide, which helps block visible light that can also darken marks
- reapplying every two hours, and after sweating or swimming
- a hat, and shade at the strongest hours, around 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.

For more on choosing one that suits your skin tone, see [sunscreen for dark skin](/guides/sunscreen-for-dark-skin).

## Step 4: one gentle evening-out ingredient

Once pimples are calmer and sunscreen is a habit, you can add one ingredient. Evening-out ingredients slow how much pigment skin makes, or help it shed marked cells. Add one at a time. Judge it over about 12 weeks.

- **Azelaic acid** is an acid that slows the enzyme skin uses to make pigment. A 16-week pilot study of a 15% gel found it reduced both acne and marks [6]. A 12-week trial of a 20% cream also saw marks improve, with more side effects than the comparison treatment in the first month [9]. See [azelaic acid on dark skin](/guides/azelaic-acid-dark-skin) for the detail.
- **Retinoids** are vitamin A relatives that also treat acne itself. A review says retinoids such as adapalene (often sold as a gel) safely treat acne-related marks in darker skin [2], and another concludes retinoids and azelaic acid are safe and beneficial in skin of colour [7]. AAD lists retinoids, including adapalene gel, among ingredients that can fade dark spots [1]. Which ones need a prescription differs by country, so ask a pharmacist. Irritation can make marks worse, so go gently [2]. If a product burns or stings, stop using it [1].

For most people, results are partial. A review of 48 studies found partial improvement in 85% of people using topical retinoids, and concluded that no treatment worked strongly and reliably for everyone [5].

> **Safety:** If you use a retinoid, note that NHS medicines guidance advises against topical retinoids in pregnancy or when planning a pregnancy, as a precaution [10]. Ask a pharmacist or doctor if you are pregnant or breastfeeding.

For the full routine, including other ingredients and the order to use them, read [how to fade dark marks on dark skin](/guides/fade-dark-marks-dark-skin).

## Can dark spots clear in 2 days?

If you have a date, such as graduation, it makes sense to look for something fast. But there is no honest two-day fix. The research on treatments measures change over 12 to 16 weeks [6][9], and the typical result is partial [5]. AAD says some imported skin products have been found to contain undisclosed steroids or mercury [1], so be cautious of any cream that promises overnight results.

A realistic aim before a deadline is no new marks, calmer skin and older marks visibly fainter. See [how long dark spots take to fade](/guides/how-long-dark-spots-take-to-fade) for what to expect week by week.

## If you hoped for lighter skin

Many people searching this want skin that looks lighter, and that wish is understandable. Your natural skin colour is yours, and no routine changes it.

What a good routine can do is fade the marks, calm the skin and give you an even tone in your own colour. That is a result you can reach safely.

> **Safety:** Skin-lightening creams are a risk, because some imported products have been found to contain steroids or mercury that are not on the label [1].

## When to see a professional

See a doctor (GP), pharmacist or dermatologist if:

- you have lots of spots, or spots that are deep, painful or leaving scars [3]
- the skin looks hot, swollen and red, or you see signs of infection [3]
- acne is affecting your mood or mental health [3][4]
- acne suddenly gets worse, or over-the-counter products have not worked [4]
- a spot looks unusual. Not all spots are acne [4]
- marks are not fading after a few months of a gentle routine. A dermatologist has stronger treatments [1]
- you are pregnant or breastfeeding and want to treat acne or marks

Early treatment of acne helps avoid permanent scarring [4]. If a specialist is hard to reach, a pharmacist is a good first step.
